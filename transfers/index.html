Door to door barely pays

A carton is picked, packed, shipped, received and re-ranged by hand, and it costs the same whether it holds three units or thirty

No door-to-door transfer clears its own cost

13,272 candidate moves were tested and every one was declined — not because the margin is too thin, but because the surplus is too dispersed. No single door has enough spare to fill a carton, so the fixed cost of raising one lands on a handful of units. This is the finding, not a gap: at this grain the direct lane is the wrong route, which is what the next section is for.

158 doors each give back a few units, which no direct lane can economically carry, and 84 doors receive them as full cartons. That costs £8 a unit — 158 cartons in and 84 out, four handling touches rather than two, and freight on both legs — and it protects £12,093 of margin for £3,383 of cost. The price is time: the stock is not sellable again until W20, so this decision has a deadline rather than a discussion.

Feeding a door that is missing most of its run is throwing good units after stranded ones: the customer who walks in cannot be fitted, so the units sell on discount or not at all. Consolidating into fewer doors that still hold a complete run restores full-price selling in fewer places, which is worth more than thin coverage everywhere — and it is the recommendation a rebalancing report usually omits, because it reads as reducing distribution.

Move it or mark it down

A discount is a decision about price, and it should not be taken before the cheaper decision about place has been ruled out

What each route is worth, and what is left for the discountCortex
Recovering 417 units at full price protects £12,093 of margin for £3,383 of logistics. The same units discounted at 20% where they stand would recover £0 — which is the comparison, and it is why place is decided before price. What no route reaches is 417 units: 0 stranded in sizes their door does not range and 417 too dispersed to be worth collecting. Those are the honest markdown candidates, and they are a much smaller markdown than the one the imbalance would otherwise justify.
“Should we move Otto Cardigan or discount it?”
Move it, then discount what is left. 417 units are in doors that will not sell them, and 417 of those are recoverable at full price — but not door to door, where 13,272 candidate moves fail the operational floor. Consolidating through the DC costs £3,383 and protects £12,093. That leaves 417 units for the markdown rather than the whole imbalance.

Semantic view: CALLA.MERCH.SV_TRANSFER_POSITION
-- Door × size imbalance, and what each route can recover
WITH position AS (
  SELECT
    st.LOCATION_ID,
    l.CLUSTER_ID,
    st.SIZE_CODE,
    SUM(st.CLOSING_STOCK)                       AS stock_on_hand,
    SUM(st.UNITS_SOLD)                          AS units_sold
  FROM CALLA.MERCH.FCT_TRADING_WEEK st
  JOIN CALLA.MERCH.DIM_LOCATION l USING (LOCATION_ID)
  WHERE st.STYLE_CODE = 'KN-CDG-009'
    AND st.ISO_WEEK   = 18
  GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
),
demand AS (
  SELECT
    p.*,
    -- Demand is the door's own profile share of its own volume, not a pro-rata
    -- split: pro-rata is what put the stock in the wrong place.
    SUM(p.units_sold) OVER (PARTITION BY p.LOCATION_ID) * sp.DEMAND_SHARE
      AS demand_per_week
  FROM position p
  JOIN CALLA.MERCH.BRG_LOCATION_SIZE_PROFILE b
    ON b.LOCATION_ID = p.LOCATION_ID
  JOIN CALLA.MERCH.DIM_SIZE_PROFILE sp
    ON sp.SIZE_PROFILE_ID = b.SIZE_PROFILE_ID
   AND sp.SIZE_CODE       = p.SIZE_CODE
)
SELECT
  LOCATION_ID,
  CLUSTER_ID,
  SIZE_CODE,
  stock_on_hand,
  ROUND(demand_per_week, 1)                                     AS demand_per_week,
  ROUND(stock_on_hand / NULLIF(demand_per_week, 0), 1)          AS cover_weeks,
  GREATEST(0, ROUND(demand_per_week * 4) - stock_on_hand)       AS deficit_units,
  GREATEST(0, stock_on_hand - ROUND(demand_per_week * 7))       AS surplus_units,
  -- A size the door does not range has no demand at any depth
  IFF(sp_ranged.SIZE_CODE IS NULL, stock_on_hand, 0)            AS stranded_units
FROM demand
LEFT JOIN CALLA.MERCH.DIM_SIZE_PROFILE sp_ranged
       ON sp_ranged.SIZE_PROFILE_ID = demand.SIZE_PROFILE_ID
      AND sp_ranged.SIZE_CODE       = demand.SIZE_CODE
      AND sp_ranged.IS_RANGED
ORDER BY deficit_units DESC, surplus_units DESC;
